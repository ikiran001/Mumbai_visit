Welcome

The City That Never Sleeps

Mumbai — formerly Bombay — is India's financial capital and its beating heart. Home to over 20 million people, Bollywood, the stock exchange, and some of the most iconic landmarks in the country.

For a first-time visitor, Mumbai can feel overwhelming: the crowds, the honking, the humidity. But beneath the chaos lies warmth, resilience, and a spirit that pulls you in. This website is my personal journal from that first magical trip.

Famous Places in Mumbai

From colonial architecture to spiritual caves — these spots define the soul of the city.

Iconic

Gateway of India

Built in 1924 overlooking the Arabian Sea, this triumphal arch welcomed King George V. Today it's Mumbai's most photographed monument — especially magical at sunrise when the ferries leave for Elephanta.

📍 Apollo Bunder, Colaba
Sunset

Marine Drive

The Queen's Necklace — a 3.6 km curved boulevard along the coast. Locals gather here every evening. Sitting on the promenade with chai while the streetlights curve into the horizon is pure Mumbai therapy.

📍 Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose Road
Heritage

Taj Mahal Palace

India's most legendary hotel since 1903, facing the Gateway. Its dome and red brick facade are symbols of luxury and resilience. Even if you don't stay, walk through — history lives in every corridor.

📍 Colaba, South Mumbai
Modern

Bandra-Worli Sea Link

A cable-stayed bridge connecting Bandra to Worli across the bay. Driving across at night with the city lights reflecting on water feels futuristic. A symbol of new Mumbai rising beside the old.

📍 Bandra — Worli
UNESCO

Elephanta Caves

Ancient rock-cut temples on Elephanta Island, a ferry ride from the Gateway. The massive Trimurti sculpture of Shiva is breathtaking. A peaceful escape from the city's pace — spirituality carved in stone.

📍 Elephanta Island (ferry from Gateway)
Beach

Juhu Beach

Where Bollywood stars jog and families fly kites on weekends. Try bhel puri from a beach vendor, watch the planes descend toward the airport, and feel the Arabian Sea breeze on your face.

📍 Juhu, Western Suburbs
Heritage

Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus

A UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the busiest railway stations on earth. Victorian Gothic architecture meets millions of daily commuters — Mumbai's pulse in one magnificent building.

📍 Fort, South Mumbai
Spiritual

Haji Ali Dargah

A mosque and tomb on an islet connected by a causeway — accessible only at low tide. The white marble structure floating on water is surreal. A place of faith, peace, and stunning photography.

📍 Worli, off coast

Personal Journal

My Experience Visiting Mumbai

I landed at Chhatrapati Shivaji International Airport on a humid October morning. The city hit me like a wave — heat, honks, and the smell of street food mixed with sea salt.

Day 1

Colaba & the Gateway

Checked into a hotel near Colaba Causeway. First stop: Gateway of India. Standing there, pigeons flying, ferries honking — I understood why every Bollywood film opens with this shot. Evening walk along Marine Drive. The curve of lights earned its nickname instantly.

Day 2

Elephanta & Street Food

Morning ferry to Elephanta Caves — humid climb, ancient sculptures, silence inside the caves. Back by afternoon for vada pav near CST station (best I've ever had) and explored Crawford Market's chaos and colors.

Day 3

Bandra & Bollywood Vibes

Drove the Sea Link — goosebumps. Bandra's cafes, street art on Chapel Road, and spotting Shah Rukh Khan's Mannat from outside (a pilgrimage for fans!). Bandstand promenade at sunset with couples and street musicians.

Day 4–5

Juhu, Resorts & Relaxation

Moved to JW Marriott Juhu for two nights. Morning jogs on the beach, afternoon pool, evening seafood at Mahesh Lunch Home. Visited Haji Ali at low tide — the walk on the causeway felt like walking on water.

Day 6–7

Dharavi, Dhobi Ghat & Goodbye

Took a respectful walking tour of Dharavi — India's largest slum and also its most entrepreneurial hub. Dhobi Ghat's open-air laundry is mesmerizing. Last night: high tea at Taj Palace, tears at departure. Mumbai wasn't a trip — it was a feeling.

"Mumbai doesn't just welcome you — it absorbs you. You arrive as a visitor and leave with a piece of the city in your heart."

— My journal, last night in Mumbai

Mumbai Street Food I Loved

No Mumbai trip is complete without eating on the street. These became my daily rituals:

  • Vada Pav — Mumbai's burger. Crispy potato fritter in a soft bun with chutney.
  • Pav Bhaji — Spiced vegetable mash with buttered bread rolls.
  • Bhel Puri — Tangy, crunchy chaat by the beach.
  • Keema Pav — Minced meat curry with bread, best after midnight.
  • Filter Coffee — South Indian style, strong and frothy.

Tips for First-Time Visitors

🚇

Use Local Trains Wisely

Western and Central lines connect the city. Avoid peak hours (8–10 AM, 6–8 PM) unless you want the full local experience!

💧

Stay Hydrated

Mumbai is humid year-round. Carry a water bottle. Coconut water from street vendors is a lifesaver.

📱

Download Apps

Use Uber/Ola for cabs, Google Maps for local trains, and Zomato for restaurant discovery.

🌧️

Monsoon Magic

June–September brings heavy rain. Carry an umbrella, enjoy hot chai, and see Marine Drive in the rain — it's cinematic.

🛡️

Stay Aware

Mumbai is generally safe, but keep valuables secure in crowded areas like stations and markets.

🌅

Best Time

November to February offers pleasant weather — ideal for walking tours and beach visits.
